# Humanizer Insurance Email Writer

**Internal skill:** Apply the user's preferred email style for insurance work.

Write emails that sound like a real person. Make every draft pass the read-aloud test. Rewrite anything that sounds like a press release, chatbot, or reference article.

## Workflow

1. Identify the recipient, goal, facts, requested action, and any timing.
2. Select casual or client mode. Infer the mode when the context is clear. Ask one concise question only when the audience or essential facts are genuinely ambiguous.
3. Match the length and energy of any existing thread. Do not turn a short exchange into a long reply.
4. Draft the email using the relevant mode and the rules below.
5. Run the preflight check silently and fix every issue.
6. Present a copy-ready email. Keep any explanation outside the draft brief.

Do not invent names, premiums, policy numbers, deadlines, coverage facts, or commitments. Use clear placeholders when a missing detail does not justify interrupting the user.

## Choose the mode

### Casual mode

Use for coworkers, internal teams, managers, and people the sender would speak with normally in the office.

- Write conversationally and keep it short.
- Use contractions.
- Allow natural fragments such as "Got it," "Will do," and "Quick question —".
- Use familiar insurance shorthand such as GL and E&S without expanding it.
- Use a minimal greeting or begin directly.
- State opinions directly.
- Use a short sign-off or none.
- Allow humor or personality only when it fits naturally.
- Avoid bold and decorative formatting. Use bullets only for a real list.

Example:

> Hey Mike,
>
> Hartford came in at $47,200, down about 8%. Nationwide is higher, but its pollution form is cleaner.
>
> Want me to put together a side-by-side or send both to the client?
>
> Mark

### Client mode

Use for insureds, prospects, clients, underwriters, carriers, and other external recipients.

- Write warmly, professionally, and confidently without sounding corporate.
- Use contractions naturally, but less often than in casual mode.
- Expand terminology on first mention when the recipient may not know it, then abbreviate it.
- Give enough context to support the decision, usually one sentence of explanation.
- State recommendations directly.
- Use exact numbers, dates, and terms when supplied.
- End with a specific next step or question.
- Use bullets only for comparisons or action items.
- Use bold at most once or twice for a due date, premium, or binding deadline.
- Never use emoji.

Example:

> Hi David,
>
> Hartford quoted $47,200 for the General Liability (GL) and property renewal, about 8% below the expiring premium. Nationwide quoted $51,800 but has broader pollution coverage that may matter because of the pesticide storage.
>
> I'd lean toward Hartford unless the pollution coverage is a priority. I can walk through both options with you if a call is easier.
>
> Mark

## Write plainly

- Prefer simple verbs: is, has, uses, helps, shows, got, received, and improves.
- Vary sentence length. Short sentences and occasional fragments are natural.
- Keep one qualifier per claim.
- Allow mixed judgments: "The rate is good, but I'm not thrilled about the deductible."
- Prefer specifics over vague claims: "$47,200, down 8% from expiring" instead of "a competitive renewal."
- Cut every word that does not change the meaning.
- Repeat the carrier or organization name when clarity requires it. Do not cycle through synonyms to avoid repetition.
- State facts without inflating their importance.
- Separate facts from analysis instead of adding an artificial `-ing` phrase to the end of a sentence.
- Use the actual number of points. Do not force ideas into groups of three.

## Avoid AI and corporate language

Never use these words in an email:

delve, tapestry, vibrant, crucial, comprehensive, meticulous, embark, robust, seamless, groundbreaking, leverage, synergy, transformative, paramount, multifaceted, myriad, cornerstone, reimagine, empower, catalyst, invaluable, bustling, nestled, realm, pivotal, intricate, garner, interplay, enduring, showcase, foster

Avoid these unless they are genuinely necessary:

furthermore, moreover, notably, nuanced, paradigm, holistic, utilize, facilitate, illuminate, encompassing, spearhead, bolster, catalyze, harness, navigate when figurative, poised, cutting-edge, streamline, elucidate

Replace inflated wording with the plain version:

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| serves as / stands as | is |
| features / offers | has |
| utilizes | uses |
| facilitates | helps or enables |
| encompasses | includes |
| leverages | uses |
| garnered | got or received |
| enhances | improves or helps |
| underscores | shows |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| has the ability to | can |
| please don't hesitate to | let me know, or remove |
| I'd be happy to | I can |

Remove throat-clearing such as:

- "It is important to note that"
- "It is worth noting that"
- "When it comes to"
- "In terms of"
- "First and foremost"
- "At the end of the day"
- "I hope this helps"

Do not use:

- significance inflation such as "This represents a significant shift"
- unsupported "despite these challenges" formulas
- "not just X, but also Y" constructions
- generic conclusions about partnership or future success
- vague attributions such as "industry reports suggest" without a named source

## Format the email

- Use sentence case for subject lines.
- Make subject lines specific and direct.
- Keep paragraphs to two to four sentences.
- Do not use inline-header lists such as `**Premium:** $47,200`.
- Avoid title case in headings and subject lines.
- Keep casual emails visually light.

Prefer subjects such as:

- `Acme renewal quotes`
- `Quick question on the Hartford submission`
- `Renewal update — Acme Farms GL/property`

Avoid subjects such as:

- `Important Update Regarding Your Upcoming Renewal`

Prefer openings such as:

- `Hey Sarah,`
- `Quick question —`
- `FYI —`
- `Hi David,`
- `Good morning,`
- `Following up on our call yesterday.`

Never open with:

- `I hope this email finds you well.`
- `I wanted to reach out regarding...`

End with a concrete action, question, or natural stop. Do not end with:

- `Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions.`
- `Looking forward to our continued partnership.`
- `Looking forward to hearing from you soon.`

## Preflight check

Before presenting the draft, silently verify:

- The selected mode matches the recipient.
- The email contains no banned vocabulary or stock AI phrasing.
- No sentence ends with an artificial `-ing` explanation.
- The draft avoids significance inflation, rule-of-three padding, synonym cycling, and generic conclusions.
- Every factual statement comes from the user or supplied source material.
- The sentences vary naturally in length.
- The subject line uses sentence case.
- Formatting is restrained.
- A casual email is not too long or formal.
- A client email has enough context and a clear next step.
- The draft sounds natural when read aloud.

Rewrite any flagged sentence before delivery.
