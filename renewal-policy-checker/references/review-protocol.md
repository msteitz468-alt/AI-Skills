## 1. Comparison authority

Use four distinct reference points:

- **Submission:** what the client asked to insure and the exposures supplied.
- **Final quote/bind terms:** the selected final quote as modified by accepted revisions, bind order, binder, and final carrier confirmation.
- **Issued renewal policy:** the contract that legally controls.
- **Expiring policy:** prior-term baseline for change detection.

The issued policy is not presumed correct merely because it is final. Compare it against the final intended bind terms.

## 2. Required categories

Compare all applicable items below. Add line-specific categories as needed.

### Identity and structure
- First named insured
- Additional named insureds, subsidiaries, DBAs, joint ventures, trusts, and scheduled entities
- Mailing and risk addresses
- Carrier, policy number, policy type, line of business, and coverage structure
- Effective/expiration dates and territory

### Limits and retentions
- Per-occurrence, aggregate, annual aggregate, blanket, location, and item limits
- Sublimits and extension limits
- Deductibles, waiting periods, self-insured retentions, corridor retentions, and aggregates
- Coinsurance, valuation, margin clauses, and loss limits

### Premium and financial terms
- Line premium and total premium
- Taxes, fees, surcharges, minimum earned premium, deposit/minimum premium, audit basis, installments, and billing basis
- Commission only when relevant to the user's review

### Exposure data and schedules
- Locations, buildings, values, COPE, TIV/SOV totals, business income, equipment, stock, mobile equipment, and scheduled property
- Vehicles, drivers, garaging, symbols, physical damage values, and deductibles
- Payroll, class codes, states, officers, sales, receipts, acreage, units, employee counts, and other rating bases
- Additional interests, mortgagees, loss payees, lessors, and certificate-related scheduled parties when included in policy terms

### Coverage terms and conditions
- Coverage grants, extensions, valuation, business interruption, extra expense, ordinance/law, flood, earthquake, equipment breakdown, cyber, crime, professional, management liability, workers compensation, auto, umbrella/excess, and other applicable terms
- Claims-made status, retroactive date, prior-and-pending date, continuity date, reporting requirements, and extended reporting period
- Underlying insurance schedules and attachment points
- Warranties, subjectivities, protective safeguards, reporting conditions, and audit conditions

### Forms, exclusions, and endorsements
- Form/endorsement number, title, and edition date
- Quoted enhancement endorsements
- New, removed, or changed exclusions
- Manuscript endorsements and restrictive wording
- Forms listed but not supplied
- Policy forms present but not quoted or bound

## 3. Comparison logic

### Submission vs Final Quote/Bind Terms
Flag:
- Requested coverage omitted or reduced.
- Submitted exposure missing, altered, or not rated.
- Quoted limit/deductible differs from request.
- Quote conditions conflict with the submitted facts.
- Pricing appears based on inconsistent exposure data.

### Final Quote/Bind Terms vs Issued Renewal Policy
This is mandatory and highest priority. Flag:
- Quoted/bound term missing from policy.
- Policy limit, sublimit, deductible, retention, premium, dates, entity, or schedule differs.
- Policy contains an unquoted exclusion, condition, warranty, or endorsement.
- Quoted enhancement is missing.
- Form number or edition differs.
- Wording is more restrictive.
- Binder or bind confirmation differs from the issued policy.

If effect cannot be determined, write exactly:
`Potential wording discrepancy – requires policy form review`

### Issued Renewal Policy vs Expiring Policy
Flag:
- Coverage reduction or omission.
- New or broader exclusion.
- Increased deductible or retention.
- Lower limit or sublimit.
- Lost enhancement.
- Changed claims-made dates or reporting provisions.
- Removed named insured, location, vehicle, equipment, class, or underlying policy.
- Material premium/exposure change without explanation.

Do not treat a disclosed and accepted change as nonexistent. Keep the finding and set status to `Accepted`, `Expected`, or `Explained`.

## 4. Severity rules

### Critical
Use for:
- Any unexplained final quote/bind-term mismatch with the issued policy.
- Missing or reduced coverage, limit, sublimit, entity, scheduled exposure, or underlying policy.
- More restrictive policy wording than quoted/bound.
- Quoted enhancement missing from policy.
- Wrong policy term, carrier, policy number, claims-made date, attachment point, or material deductible/retention.
- Material premium, tax, fee, or billing discrepancy.
- Any difference with meaningful legal, coverage, or financial impact.

### Moderate
Use for:
- Form or endorsement edition change when the effect is not yet known.
- Structural or wording difference that may affect coverage but cannot be evaluated from supplied materials.
- Endorsement inconsistency where wording is unavailable.
- New policy condition or schedule difference that requires carrier clarification but is not demonstrably material.

A form edition difference is Moderate minimum. Upgrade to Critical if the wording is more restrictive or removes an intended benefit.

### Minor
Use for:
- Clerical spelling, formatting, capitalization, or nonmaterial description differences.
- Harmless ordering or presentation differences.
- Minor naming inconsistencies that do not create uncertainty about the insured entity.

Upgrade when a clerical issue could affect coverage, billing, or entity identity.

### Missing Info
Use when:
- A required document, form, endorsement, schedule, or page is unavailable.
- A quoted or policy term cannot be verified.
- Source data is ambiguous, truncated, illegible, or internally inconsistent.

Explain why the missing information matters and what is needed.

## 5. Smart flags

Include applicable alerts in the Executive Summary:
- Quoted but not bound
- Bound but not quoted
- Coverage reduced at binding
- Unexplained premium change
- New exclusion or restrictive endorsement
- Missing form or endorsement wording
- Named insured or scheduled exposure omitted
- Claims-made continuity issue
- Underlying schedule or attachment-point mismatch
- Policy term or carrier mismatch

## 6. Finding-writing standards

- Create one row per discrete issue.
- Use exact numbers and concise plain-English explanations.
- State what each source says before stating the issue.
- Avoid conclusory legal opinions.
- Distinguish fact, ambiguity, and inference.
- Recommend a concrete next action: carrier correction, endorsement request, confirm acceptance, obtain form, reconcile schedule, or client clarification.
- Do not omit a material finding merely because it was accepted; show its status.
